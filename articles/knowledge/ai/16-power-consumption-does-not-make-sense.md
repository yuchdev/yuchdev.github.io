Power consumption matters, but it is far from the main measure of economic efficiency. The relevant economic metric is the cost per unit of useful work, or value created.

The Deep Blue comparison is quite weak: it uses 1997 hardware and compares an entire machine with only the brain's metabolic consumption. Modern chess engines that outperform DeepBlue (and any human) run on smartphone.

The drone point is narrower but valid: large general-purpose models are unsuitable for small battery-constrained platforms. But that does not mean "AI cannot run on drones"; lightweight vision models and specialized accelerators already do.

Human brains remain vastly more energy-efficient than current computers. Yet lower energy efficiency abdolutely does not imply economic non-viability. Cars use more energy than walking, excavators more than shovels, and datacenters more than filing cabinets. They remain economical when the additional output exceed their total cost.
