### AI IS DYNAMIC MARKET AND REQUIRES CONSTANT CATCH-UP

"Learning AI tools" is no longer just learning ChatGPT prompts. Becoming genuinely productive with Claude, Codex, Copilot, Junie, MCP servers, multi-agentic workflows, CI integration, guardrails and evaluation, and skills-driven development is already a specialization in itself.

Experienced engineers will remain valuable. But the market is undeniably shrinking while expectations are rising. Layoffs, higher hiring bars, and declining median salaries are no longer isolated events - they're global industry-wide reality.

The developers who will fall behind are not just "who doesn't know AI", but those who don't find a niche where they create value in the nearest 5 years.

That niche might be:

- deep business domain expertise (banking, medtech, telecom)
- system/kernel programming and performance engineering
- security and compliance
- distributed systems
- AI orchestration and evaluation
- Areas where human hands matter (embedded, drones, robotics)
- cross-domain expertise (C++/Computer Vision, Java/Finance, Python/ML).

AI writes code. Humans are still needed to define problems, negotiate requirements, design architectures, understand regulation, debug production failures, and make engineering decisions.

---

Keeping up with modern AI has become a discipline in itself. For example, Claude's official certification course alone is around 12 hours of dense technical material, not counting practice. And by the time you finish it, they already shipped several new capabilities.

Today you're expected to understand not just "prompt engineering", but also developing agents, skills, MCP, loops, structured outputs, memory architectures, RAG, evaluation frameworks (evals), model routing, reasoning and caching, local models and work with Hugging Face, vLLM/llama, vector databases, token optimization, CI AI agents and agent orchestration.

Unlike Java (roughly yearly releases) or C++ (every three years), the AI ecosystem evolves weekly. Staying current is effectively a continuous education stream.

So yes, staying sharp as a developer today means both keeping your programming/CS fundamentals strong and continuously learning AI tooling. Neither replaces the other.
