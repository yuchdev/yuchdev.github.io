There is a difference in mechanism, but not necessarily in outcome.

You can expect misinformation from the internet.
You can expect misinformation from an LLM.
In both cases, the safe habit is the same: don't treat a single source as truth.

With human-written content, we usually reduce risk by checking multiple sources, looking at author reputation, comments, reviews, quotes, and practical evidence. With LLMs, the same principle applies: ask for sources, verify against independent material, and be especially careful when the answer sounds confident but cannot be checked. Of course, the fact-check can be automated.

If we want to design a misinformation-resistant AI workflow, one approach at least I recommend is to use not a single agent, but a multi-agent pipeline: one agent proposes the answer, another challenges it or searches for counterexamples, a third acts as moderator/fact-checker and decides what survives.

It reduces the chance that one plausible false claim passes through unchallenged by orders of magnitude.

Just like with analyzing multiple sources on the internet.
