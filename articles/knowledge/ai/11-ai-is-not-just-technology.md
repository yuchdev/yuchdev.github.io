I don't think the comparison with compilers is accurate. They changed software engineering in fundamentally different ways

The first FORTRAN compilers didn't make programmers 1000x more productive. Early compilers often generated quite slow code, and engineers distrusted them for years. Their main contribution was raising the level of abstraction

LLM change something else entirely. They don't translate one language into another - they create app designs, generate source code, explain APIs, write tests, and review PRs

A compiler is deterministic. Given the same source, it always produces the same result

An LLM is probabilistic. Given the same prompt, it may produce different solutions

I'm also pretty sure the introduction of compilers did not trigger a global hype cycle, a vibe-coding movement, hundreds of thousands of layoffs across the tech industry, a fundamental reshaping of the job market, or an explosion of entirely new AI-native products and companies

That's the paradigm shift. Compilers automated translation from high-level languages to machine code. LLMs automate an engineering process itself. I can't think of another tool that transformed software engineering, hiring, and the industry at this speed and on this scale.

---

What I noticed, humans (engineers, specifically) keep framing AI like some new software technology or development concept, like compiled languages or OOP, which is fundamentally wrong.

It is not just another compiler, framework, or database. It is not even comparable to a major industrial technology, even transfornimg one like the steam engine, or even the industrial revolution itself, because those technologies mostly extended human muscle, speed, logistics, manufacturing, or communication.

AI interferes with the human cognition itself - that makes it historically unique. The steam engine transformed labor. Electricity transformed production and daily life. The internet transformed communication and access to information. But AI introduces between human intention and action, no matter who this human is - an engineer, a lawyer or a doctor.

Together with Neuralink-style brain-computer interfaces and other neurotechnologies, AI may become more than an economic or technological shift. It may become a first artificial evolutionary driver: a new layer between biological intelligence, machine intelligence, memory and society.

In that sense, the better comparisons may be fire, agriculture, writing, or language itself - technologies that changed not only what humans could do, but what humans could become.

And arguing whether AI is going to take our jobs is like guessing whether the fire makes mammoth hunters weaker. We're still in the beginning of transformation, no one knows where equilibrium will settle.
