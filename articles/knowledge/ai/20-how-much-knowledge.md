I wonder how many adults still remember how to do long division by hand.

There's a satirical sci-fi story I vaguely remember: a modern person somehow ends up in the Middle Ages and tries to prove he is from the future. The problem is that, without a smartphone and internet access, he cannot actually reproduce much "future knowledge" himself.

He knows that electricity exists, that antibiotics work, that airplanes fly, that computers are possible - but knowing that something exists is very different from knowing enough to build it from first principles.

We have outsourced enormous parts of our practical knowledge first to calculators, then search engines, and now AI. The interesting question is how much knowledge we should retain personally so that we can still reason, verify, and reconstruct things when the tool is unavailable.
