Unfortunately, the strategic horizon of many companies is not "what happens to the economy in ten years?" It is "can we show steady growth to shareholders next quarter?"

That creates a very dangerous prospective. If replacing people with AI improves margins now, the broader consequences become someone else's problem later - possibly under different management, after the current executives have already collected their bonuses.

And many of the loudest AI evangelists will not be around to clean up the damage. They will present the transformation, sell the productivity story, and jump out with a "golden parachute" in the right moment, before the social and economic costs fully arrive.
