### IT TAKES A YEAR TO ENTER INDUSTRY

I'd argue that entering the profession already takes 9-10 months at best - and that's not about the language alone.

A junior developer needs to learn not just language (any language), but:

- how the OS actually works (processes, memory, files)
- the toolchain (depends on language)
- the surrounding ecosystem (standard library, 3rd-party libs)
- basic engineering practices (debugging, testing, logging, version control)

For a language as complex as C++, one year is usually enough only to start working under close supervision of a senior developer.

Becoming productive independently is a different milestone. That typically takes a couple more years: learning to reason about performance, understanding failure modes and undefined behavior, making safe architectural decisions, and knowing when not to "be too clever" wherever possible. 

So yes - the future developer may learn a lot of C++ in a year.
But becoming a trusted, autonomous C++ engineer is a longer journey, and there's no real shortcut around that.
