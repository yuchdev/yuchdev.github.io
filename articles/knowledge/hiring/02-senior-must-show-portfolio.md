### SENIOR ENGINEER MUST SHOW SOME PORTFOLIO

In medium-to-large companies we usually went the long way on evaluation - intentionally. Because if you try to "save" on properly evaluating a senior engineer, you almost always pay much more later.

Startups are different - requirements there and actually stricter, not looser:

- The cost of one hour of a senior engineer is dramatically higher than in enterprise
- There is no buffer: no spare teams, no process padding, no time to "grow into the role"
- Every architectural mistake compounds into technical debt immediately

That's why in startups we often had to skip candidates who can't demonstrate theur work altogether. And yes - I strongly believe a senior candidate should have at least one portfolio project (public, demo, or pet-project).

Not because "open source is good", but because I don't see other way to evaluate how candidate consider testing, documentation, design trade-offs - not just syntax, or how to separate "worked on" from "understood and designed"

### WHY YOU DON'T HAVE ANYTHING TO SHOW?

That's exactly why I never quite understand senior-level developers with a decade of experience and no code to show as a portfolio.
Where do they learn and safely test new ideas - new languages, paradigms, frameworks, architectures? On a company's production code? That's an extremely expensive place to experiment, both technically and organizationally.

Personally, I can't go more than a few days without the itch to try some new idea. Sometimes it stays a sketch. Sometimes it becomes a prototype. And sometimes it finds its way into real systems and bring real value.
