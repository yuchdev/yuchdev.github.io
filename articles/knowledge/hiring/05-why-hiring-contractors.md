### WHY HIRING CONTRACTORS

Hiring contractors is an absolutely viable business decision - and in many cases the correct one.

Full-time roles make sense when you're building long-lived product knowledge and sustained ownership. But there are plenty of situations where permanent headcount is the wrong metric.

Examples I've seen work extremely well with contractors:

Highly specialized, time-boxed tasks:

Cross-platform installers, build and packaging systems, legacy migrations - skills you need deeply for a few months, not forever.

Independent validation:

Security audits, performance reviews, architectural assessments - areas where external distance is a feature, not a bug.

Knowledge injection:

Bringing in experts to train a team on a new technology, help establish best practices, and leave behind documentation and guardrails that actually stick.

Where it goes wrong is when contractors are treated as second-class contributors or isolated from the team. If they're contributing to delivery, they need the same access, context, and respect - otherwise you pay for expertise and then systematically prevent it from compounding.

Like most things in engineering leadership, it's not about the model - it's about how consistently you apply it.
