Some companies provide feedback, some don't. Some provide it only for certain candidates or later-stage interviews. I've learned to treat it philosophically, mostly because I've been on both sides of the table many times.

I still remember my first interview as a team lead. I gave a candidate detailed feedback: what was strong, what was weak, and what I thought he should improve. He immediately started arguing that the weak points were not actually weak - and, in general, were not important in C++ anyway 😀

So while I personally appreciate constructive feedback, I also understand why some interviewers avoid giving it. Even well-intended feedback can turn into a debate, a complaint, or an HR risk.

In the end, every interview is still useful experience. Even without explicit feedback, you can usually learn something: which questions surprised you, where your explanation was weak, where you lacked confidence, and what kind of role or company may not be the right fit.
