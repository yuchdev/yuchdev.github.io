Promoting the best engineer to management without validating leadership skills is a management failure, not an engineering failure.

Years ago, when I was acting as a CTO, I had a genuinely brilliant engineer on the team. Exceptional technical depth, solved problems others couldn't, enormous productivity. But he was also mildly autistic and had little interest in people, conflict resolution, mentoring, or organizational politics.

If I had promoted him into a decision-making leadership role simply because he was the strongest engineer, I would likely have lost the entire team within months.

Being a great engineer and being a great manager are different professions.

The bigger problem is that many organizations still treat management as the "next level" after engineering. As a result, they accidentally punish excellence: the best engineers are pushed into jobs they don't want and aren't prepared for.

A stronger approach is to have parallel career tracks. Let exceptional engineers become Staff, Principal, or Architect, with compensation and influence comparable to managers.

A promotion should recognize the skills required for the target role, not reward success in a completely different one.
