### LANGUAGE EXCHANGE GOOD IDEAS

Language exchange a pretty natural and healthy process.

Modern languages constantly compete in a good sense and enrich each other, and everyone benefits from it. 

Safer idioms isn't "copying" so much as converging on patterns that proved themselves in practice.

Same story with, today's Java 21-24 is no worse than Kotlin for most production work, precisely because of this kind of pressure. Over the last years Java picked up ideas Kotlin (once way superior JVM language) popularized: records, pattern matching, sealed classes, local type inference, ongoing work around nullability annotations.

That's not collaboration in an official sense - it's just language creators doing its job.

And then there's Go, which needed about a decade to adopt generics 😀
Competition works there too, just on a geological timescale.
