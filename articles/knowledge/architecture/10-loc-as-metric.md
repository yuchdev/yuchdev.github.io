LOC is rarely used in isolation. On its own it's almost meaningless.

In practice it's combined with other metrics, for example:

- defect density (bugs per KLOC)
- code churn (added/removed LOC over time)
- cyclomatic or cognitive complexity
- test coverage
- maintainability index

A thousand well-structured lines can be far easier to maintain than a hundred overly clever ones.

The mistake isn't using LOC. The mistake is treating it as a productivity metric.
