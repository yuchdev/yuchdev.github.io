### AUTISTIC DEVELOPER AND THE TEAM

I'll share a story from my side of the table.

I once had a developer on my team who had serious communication difficulties. Not a misanthrope - just extremely introverted, and possibly mildly autistic

I wasn't the one who hired him.
And yes, there were colleagues who suggested we "solve the problem" quickly and kick him out

I didn't

Instead, I looked for the right place in the workflow.

We restructured responsibilities so his tasks were:

- highly parallel
- maximally detached from daily team interaction
- shielded from unnecessary meetings and spontaneous discussions (I really can talk to people patiently and to the point :)

Once that pressure was removed, his output became exceptional. As a coder, he was extremely strong - precise, focused, and deeply analytical. By protecting him from the parts of the job that drained him, we got the best of what he could offer

The moral for me was simple:
before letting someone go because they don't fit a standard interaction pattern, it's worth exhausting the possibility that they simply need a different role shape.

Teams are systems. Not every valuable component needs to be chatty - some just need the right operating conditions
