### ZX SPECTRUM

Historically, ZX Spectrum clones were essentially the only affordable home computers in the early 90s (ex-)USSR. That was my entry point too. My parents bought me a Speccy when I was in 3rd grade.
Assembly was way too hard at that age, but Sinclair BASIC wasn't. That's where my first real line of code happened.
I still remember the moment at school when we were asked the classic question:
"Who do you want to be when you grow up?" When kids answered doctor or pilot, I said "programmer" - and both teacher and kids gave me square eyes 😁

What's also fascinating is that for a while there was a parallel computing universe in the ex-USSR: Z80/Z280/Z380-based machines with 1-2 MB of RAM, hard drives, modems - absolute monsters by Spectrum standards. They were real systems, not toys, and for a short time they looked like a viable future.

Of course, by 1994, reality won,
x86 PCs became affordable, and the whole Z80 ecosystem was pushed aside almost overnight.

### ZX SPECTRUM 2 (HALF-JOKE)

When I was in 3rd grade, my parents bought me a ZX Spectrum - basically the only affordable home computer on the early post-Soviet market

To buy a tape for ZX Spectrum, we went to the radio market. In winter, -20 °C. Uphill in both directions.

After attempting to load Elite 25 times, you started to suspect that maybe you were not destined to play this game in this lifetime.

To play a game, you first had to write the game.

With a shortage of free tapes, you had to write it again every time.

With no save support, you had to hard-code the save system directly into BASIC - preferably between line 9000 and 9999, where "nothing important lives"

Debugging meant changing one line of BASIC, waiting 7 minutes for the tape to load again, and discovering you fixed the wrong bug - but introduced two new ones.

Multitasking meant listening carefully to the tape player and praying nobody opened the fridge, because voltage fluctuations.

Cloud storage was your cassette, clearly labeled "DO NOT TOUCH!!!", which your friend would absolutely overwrite with disco music the next day.

Copy protection was the fact that nobody else had the patience to load your program twice.

Good times.
Character-building times.
Times that explain everything about modern engineers who grew up in 90s
