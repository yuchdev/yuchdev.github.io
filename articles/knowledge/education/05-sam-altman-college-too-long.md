Sure. College is obviously far too long.

Doctors can be prepared through a three-month weekend bootcamp. Neurosurgeons may need four months, but only because of all that unnecessary hands-on practice.

A physics PhD can be replaced with an online certificate and a sufficiently confident LinkedIn profile. Civil engineers can learn bridge design from a 12-hour YouTube playlist. Chemists can skip the laboratory entirely and just ask an LLM whether the mixture is supposed to explode.

And mathematics is especially wasteful: why spend years learning proofs when you can simply prompt, "be rigorous"?

At some point, though, we may discover that education is not merely an inefficient delivery mechanism for information. Some professions require years because expertise is built through repeated practice, failure, feedback, and gradually accumulated judgment.

But yes, perhaps the real problem with universities is that they still haven't discovered the `2x playback speed` button.
